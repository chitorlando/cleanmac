CleanMac 1.0.0

The first stable release of CleanMac.

What's new

  • Security: checks, without changing anything, whether FileVault, the firewall, Gatekeeper, System Integrity Protection and automatic security updates are on, and whether XProtect is up to date. It warns about apps that are unsigned or have a broken signature and about suspicious launch agents, and lists the apps with sensitive permissions, such as Full Disk Access or Screen Recording. It isn't an antivirus.
  • CleanMac in the menu bar: CPU, memory, free space and the processes using the most CPU, one click away. It also shows how much the Trash holds and empties it with “Empty Trash…”. In Settings you can remove it or show CPU usage there.
  • With Full Disk Access, System Junk also finds your Trash and the Trash on external drives, and your iPhone and iPad backups. None of them is selected for you, and the most recent backup of each device is marked so you keep it.
  • A Settings window (CleanMac › Settings…, ⌘,): the app's language, the Monitor's refresh rate, the minimum size for Large Files and Duplicates, and excluded folders those two features never look in.
  • The Disk Map shows all your space: your Library and hidden items too. Folders macOS protects are marked, with a shortcut to the Full Disk Access settings, and iCloud files are never downloaded.
  • A welcome screen on first launch to grant Full Disk Access and Finder automation. You can return to it from CleanMac › Permissions….
  • “Show Login Items” in an app's menu lists only that app's items, and the menu of cache and log folders offers “Review in System Junk”.
  • Menus no longer offer to find duplicates or large files in folders those features don't look in.
  • Permissions now survive updates: releases are signed with a stable certificate of their own. Coming from 0.1.0, you'll grant them one last time.

Safer

  • CleanMac asks before moving anything to the Trash in the Uninstaller, System Junk, Privacy, Large Files, Duplicates and Login Items, also when you press Return.
  • Emptying the Trash, which can't be undone, always asks first, and there Return cancels.
  • System Junk leaves the caches of open browsers unselected and locked, as Privacy already did, including editions such as Beta, Canary or Nightly.
  • Clear messages when macOS protects an item, with buttons to show it in Finder or open the Full Disk Access settings. The Uninstaller marks such leftovers before moving them.

Fixes

  • Monitor: memory is shown in the same units as Activity Monitor, the first reading after coming back no longer averages the time away, and several windows no longer shorten the history.
  • Login Items: the “Disabled” badge reflects the system's real state, and an agent is only marked orphaned when its program is gone, also when an interpreter launches it.
  • Space freed is no longer counted twice when Privacy and System Junk share a cache.

Free and open source. Distributed without Apple notarization.
